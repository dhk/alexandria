Task
Test the claim that ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) is the right tool for clear, concise, persuasive writing. STE is currently cited with enthusiasm as a general-purpose writing standard, including in Claude skills that implement it — treat that enthusiasm as the thing under examination, not as a premise. Establish what STE-100 was actually built for, by whom, when, and to solve what problem; what it demonstrably does well; and where applying it outside its origin degrades the writing, with particular attention to persuasion, voice, and rhetorical effect. Then contrast it with the other techniques available for the same jobs — plain-language standards (ISO 24495-1, plain English movements), classical and modern rhetoric, Gopen & Swan reader-expectation theory, Williams' clarity-and-grace tradition, the readability-formula tradition and its documented misuse, other controlled languages, argumentation theory, and the empirical persuasion literature. Steelman STE rather than knocking it down: state the strongest version of the case for it before testing it. Distinguish what is supported by evidence from what is practitioner consensus and what is folklore, and say plainly which is which.

Context
Commissioned by DHK, who builds Claude writing skills and writes publicly, and is deciding what to recommend and what to build. Two things depend on it: a proposed set of extensions to the claude-writing-skills repo (a prior investigation, 2026-08-12-writing-communication-best-practices, found that repo's STE skill to be its most standards-grounded but noted it encodes STE's rules without its approved-word dictionary), and his own writing practice. The prior investigation also found the CV > 0.4 sentence-variance threshold used in that repo to be a heuristic with no readability-research grounding, and flagged a missing context router — meaning nothing currently tells a writer which technique to reach for. That gap is what this brief is meant to close. He wants an opinionated answer with the reasoning shown, not a survey that lists everything and recommends nothing.

Constraints


Output needs
A document that stands on its own for a reader who has not seen this brief, in this order: (1) THE CLAIM, stated precisely as it circulates, and the verdict on it up front. (2) ORIGIN AND MOTIVATION of STE-100 — who created it, when, under what commercial and safety pressures, what it optimises for, and what its own documentation explicitly disclaims about general writing. (3) WHAT THE EVIDENCE SHOWS it does well and badly, separating comprehension, translation, safety-criticality, and persuasion; name where evidence is strong, thin, or absent. (4) CONTRAST with the alternatives — for each technique: what it optimises, what it assumes about the reader, its evidence base, its failure modes, and what it costs to apply. A comparison table is expected. (5) SELECTION GUIDANCE — a usable decision procedure for choosing between them by purpose (inform, instruct, persuade, narrate, decide), audience (expert, lay, second-language, mixed, hostile), and context (safety-critical, regulatory, marketing, internal memo, public essay), including where techniques should be combined or sequenced rather than chosen between. This section is the point of the brief. (6) WHY NOW — the origin and motivation of the current enthusiasm for STE specifically, including whether it is being adopted for reasons connected to what it was built for. (7) WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS ANSWER — the open questions, and the evidence that would settle them. Cite specific standards, papers, and editions rather than gesturing at "research shows".
