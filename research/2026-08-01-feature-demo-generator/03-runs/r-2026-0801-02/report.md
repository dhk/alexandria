## Comparative Assessment: RFC-0001 Feature Demo Generator

### Areas of Strong Consensus

All three outputs converge on the same core structural conclusions with notable consistency.

**The wedge is real but narrow.** All three models independently identify that the defensible seam is not demo generation per se (crowded) nor PR summarization (very crowded), but the specific join of merge-trigger + structured PM interview + demo spec as intermediate representation. Model 2 articulates this most precisely; Models 1 and 3 reach the same conclusion through slightly different framings.

**Script-first MVP is the right scope.** All three outputs independently recommend deferring capture and video to later phases and validating only the script-generation + interview loop first. This is one of the most consistent findings across the three.

**Hybrid architecture is preferred.** All three models recommend a hybrid: CI handles repo interrogation and interview triggering automatically; visual capture is a separate, deferrable, opt-in action. This directly addresses the tension the RFC identifies between automation (fragile) and interactive invocation (skippable).

**Pre-fill is mandatory; confident wrongness is the critical failure mode.** All three outputs stress that every interview field must be pre-filled from repo/PR facts. Model 2 goes furthest in arguing that the tool should explicitly prefer "I don't know" over a hallucinated answer, particularly for customer segment and benefit fields, because a single confident-wrong pre-fill destroys trust faster than a blank field would.

**Downstream reuse is the validation signal.** All three models identify that generating artifacts nobody uses is the modal failure mode. Models 2 and 3 both propose a ~30% downstream-reuse threshold as a Phase 1 kill criterion.

**Capture fragility is the dominant technical risk.** Auth, seed data, non-deterministic UI state, and selector brittleness are flagged by all three as the reason automated headless capture should be deferred.

---

### Meaningful Divergences

**Depth of prior-art analysis.** Model 2 provides the most granular competitor map, including specific products (CodeRabbit, Graphite, Greptile, Ellipsis, Chromatic, Percy, Argos, Remotion, Danger.js, browser-use, Anthropic Computer Use) and a structured feature matrix with explicit uncertainty flags. Model 1 provides a thorough category map but fewer specific product names. Model 3 provides the most compressed treatment, relying almost entirely on the RFC's own named tools.

**Storybook/Chromatic as a capture shortcut.** Only Model 2 identifies this adjacency: if the changed surface has a Storybook story, before/after screenshots are already being generated by visual regression tooling on every PR, making this a near-zero-cost capture path for component-level changes. Models 1 and 3 do not surface this.

**Standalone product vs. integration layer.** Only Model 2 raises the uncomfortable question of whether this could be built as a plugin on top of Supademo or Arcade's APIs rather than as a standalone product, and notes that if those APIs permit programmatic demo creation, the standalone thesis weakens. This is a material strategic question the other two outputs do not address.

**Remotion for video assembly.** Only Model 2 recommends Remotion (React-based programmatic video) as the video rendering layer, with a specific rationale: the intermediate representation is a structured script, which maps naturally to Remotion's component model.

**Danger.js as scaffolding.** Only Model 2 identifies Danger.js as directly relevant existing infrastructure for the "run something on a PR and comment back" orchestration pattern.

**Phase 0 discovery before any build.** Model 2 explicitly structures a two-week discovery phase (10 user interviews + hand-classification of 50 recent PRs) before any software is written, with a kill signal if fewer than ~15–20% of merges are demo-worthy. Model 1 proposes similar validation experiments but frames them as concurrent with a thin MVP. Model 3 does not include a pre-build discovery phase.

**Author vs. demo-owner.** Only Model 2 explicitly flags that the RFC's assumption (the PR author is the right interviewee) deserves testing, proposing it as a named experiment.

**Framing of the counter-argument.** Model 2 offers the sharpest formulation: the tool may be solving a prioritization problem with technology, and the teams disciplined enough to run a two-minute interview on every merge are already disciplined enough to make demos without it. Model 1 frames this as "workflow optimization without enough pain or budget." Model 3 frames it as "a feature, not a product" that large incumbents could replicate with a webhook update. All three are valid but Model 2's framing is most directly testable.

---

### What This Run Does Not Establish

**Current capabilities of named competitors.** All three outputs explicitly disclaim live web access. The competitor matrix cells, particularly for Arcade, Supademo, Storylane, Guidde, and Scribe, are directional inferences from training data, not verified against current product documentation or APIs. Any of these tools may have added merge triggers, API-driven demo creation, or structured interview flows since the models' training cutoffs.

**Whether any direct prior art exists.** All three models note they cannot rule out a 2024–2026 YC-batch entrant, an internal tool at a large engineering organization, or a recent product launch that occupies the exact merge-trigger + PM-interview + demo-spec wedge. The absence of a known competitor is not confirmed absence.

**Technical feasibility of agentic browser capture.** Model 2 lists Anthropic Computer Use, OpenAI Operator, Skyvern, and browser-use as potentially relevant but explicitly marks their demo-quality capture reliability as unverified. No output can assess whether these tools have matured to the point where they reduce the capture fragility risk materially.

**Market size and willingness to pay.** No output has access to buyer research, pricing benchmarks for adjacent tools, or evidence of budget allocation for demo-generation workflows. The claim that this is a "budget-worthy" pain is unverified in all three outputs.

**Whether Supademo, Arcade, or Storylane expose APIs sufficient for programmatic demo creation.** Model 2 raises this as a strategic question but cannot answer it. If yes, the build-vs-integrate calculus shifts significantly.

**Interview completion rates in practice.** The 60% completion threshold proposed by Models 2 and 3 is a reasonable hypothesis, but no empirical data on engineer compliance with merge-time prompts (beyond PR description completion rates as a rough proxy) is available in any of the three outputs.

**Whether the pre-fill quality is achievable.** Model 2 proposes an experiment (blind-rate 30 pre-filled interviews for hallucination rate, kill if >15% confident-wrong) but the baseline hallucination rate for this specific task on current LLMs is not established in any output.
