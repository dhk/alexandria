Task
Determine which writing techniques suit which kinds of organizational writing, and produce two matrices plus a decision procedure a writer can follow. TECHNIQUES to cover: ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English; BLUF and the US Army writing standard; Minto's Pyramid Principle; the Amazon six-page narrative memo and PR-FAQ; plain language per ISO 24495-1:2023; blameless postmortem convention as practised in SRE culture; classical and Toulmin-style argumentation; readability-formula optimisation as embodied by the Hemingway Editor; Hotaling's rules for concise scientific writing; and the three skills in the supplied github.com/nonatofabio/claude-writing-skills repository (ste, plainspoken, humanize). GENRES: informational brief where nobody is being asked to decide; investigation summary; research proposal; recommendation and next steps; incident notification written while facts are still moving; postmortem written after; recurring status update; decision record explaining why one option was chosen over another, read later by someone who was not present; dissent arguing against a decision or a colleague's position; and reference or onboarding documentation. Assess each technique against each genre on its merits. Where a technique is unsuited to a genre, say so and say why. Where the evidence for a technique's claims is weak, say that too.

Context
Commissioned by DHK, who builds Claude writing skills and writes publicly, and is deciding what to recommend and what to build. The supplied repository is one existing attempt at tooling in this space and should be assessed on the same terms as every other technique, neither spared nor singled out. He wants an opinionated answer with the reasoning shown, not a survey that lists everything and recommends nothing.

Constraints


Output needs
A document that stands alone for a reader who has not seen this brief, in this order. (1) THE VERDICT on whether one set of writing principles serves all organizational writing, stated up front. (2) MATRIX A — THE DEMAND: rows are the ten considerations below, columns are the ten genres, each cell stating how much that genre requires that quality on an explicit declared scale with a legend. This matrix concerns the situation, not any tool. (3) MATRIX B — THE SUPPLY: rows are the same ten considerations, columns are the techniques, each cell stating whether that technique serves, is neutral toward, or actively damages that quality, on the same declared scale. Mark cells where a technique's effect is asserted by its proponents but unsupported by evidence. (4) THE SELECTION PROCEDURE, derived by matching a genre's demand profile in Matrix A against the technique profiles in Matrix B — show the matching rule rather than asserting conclusions, and name where techniques should be combined or sequenced. (5) THE GENRES WHERE EVERY AVAILABLE TECHNIQUE FAILS, if any, and what would be needed. (6) EVIDENCE AUDIT: for each technique, whether its claims rest on experimental evidence, institutional adoption, practitioner consensus, or folklore — marked per claim rather than per technique. (7) WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS ANSWER. The ten considerations, fixed and to be used exactly as given: precision; brevity; scannability; actionability; confidence calibration, meaning conveying certainty and uncertainty faithfully rather than sounding assured; empathy and regard for the reader's stake; persuasive force; accountability, meaning whether agency is visible or hidden by agentless passive constructions; accessibility for second-language readers and translation; and durability, meaning legibility to a reader who arrives later without the surrounding context. Note where these considerations are in tension with one another.
