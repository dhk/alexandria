## Comparative Analysis Report

### Overall Convergence

All three outputs reach the same verdict — one set of writing principles does not serve all organizational genres — and share a common analytical architecture: a demand matrix (what genres require), a supply matrix (what techniques provide), and a selection procedure for matching them. The degree of agreement on individual claims is unusually high, suggesting the source materials strongly constrain the conclusions.

### Where the Outputs Agree Strongly

**The humanize force-commit defect.** All three outputs independently identify the `humanize` skill's Pass 1 instruction to force claims that commit as a defect in uncertainty-bearing genres, and all three note that the skill's scientific-papers override acknowledges the problem but scopes the fix too narrowly. Output 2 is the most precise, naming four genres where it is a defect and a fifth where it is dangerous. Output 3 provides the most explicit matrix cell (−2 on confidence calibration). Output 1 frames it as a structural limit rather than a tuning bug.

**STE damages persuasion by design.** All three outputs treat this as an established finding carried forward from a prior run in the series, not a new claim. The agreement is complete and the reasoning identical: STE removes rhetorical devices because it was designed for safety-critical procedures, not persuasion.

**Readability optimization as Goodhart failure.** All three outputs invoke Goodhart's Law explicitly and reach the same conclusion: readability scores are valid diagnostics and invalid optimization targets. Output 2 extends this to the commitment dimension — "more committed is generally better" has the same Goodhart shape as "shorter is generally better" — a formulation the other two outputs approach but do not state as crisply.

**Incident notification as the hardest genre.** All three outputs identify incident notification while facts are moving as the genre most underserved by the available techniques, and all three call for a dedicated uncertainty-preserving schema with mandatory known/unknown/next-update fields. The convergence on this specific prescription is notable.

**Status update honesty as partly outside craft's reach.** All three outputs draw the same boundary: writing conventions can make honest updates easier to produce, but cannot fix incentive structures that reward reassurance.

### Where the Outputs Diverge

**ISO 24495-1 as universal substrate.** Output 2 makes a strong, specific claim: ISO 24495-1 is the only technique in the set that scores non-negative on all ten considerations and therefore functions as a safe substrate for other techniques. Outputs 1 and 3 do not make this claim; Output 1 marks ISO as largely absent from its supplied materials and assigns question marks throughout its ISO column. This is a genuine divergence, not a framing difference.

**Severity of the humanize defect.** Output 2 is the most aggressive, calling the three Claude skills "not a coherent toolkit" and explicitly contradicting any earlier suggestion that they form one. Output 1 reaches a similar conclusion ("conflicting toolkit") but frames it as a design observation rather than a contradiction of prior work. Output 3 is the least emphatic on this point.

**Evidence grading for STE accessibility.** Output 2 marks STE's L2 accessibility claims as ‡ (proponent claim outruns evidence) and explicitly states the empirical base is industry adoption, not controlled comprehension studies. Output 3 notes "some supporting studies on reduced ambiguity." Output 1 marks it P/F. The disagreement is modest but real.

**The CV > 0.4 target in humanize.** Only Output 2 explicitly flags the coefficient-of-variation sentence-rhythm target as a number chosen empirically rather than derived from evidence. Output 3 mentions the four passes but does not evaluate the specific number. Output 1 does not address it.

**Toulmin as uniquely suited to dissent.** Output 2 states that only Toulmin scores well on both persuasive force and empathy for the dissent genre. Output 1 recommends Toulmin for dissent but does not make the exclusivity claim. Output 3 agrees on Toulmin but frames it as one element of a combination.

### What This Run Does Not Establish

- **Whether any technique causally improves organizational decision outcomes.** All three outputs note that the evidence base consists of institutional adoption and practitioner consensus, not controlled experiments measuring decision quality, trust calibration, incident recurrence, or durability of reasoning. The matrices are built on proxies, not outcomes.
- **Whether Amazon's prose-over-bullets claim holds outside Amazon.** All three outputs flag this as the single most consequential unproven claim in the input set. No output has evidence to resolve it.
- **Whether mechanical audits can be modified to distinguish weasel words from confidence intervals.** All three outputs identify this as the crux of the humanize defect and note that no current tool in the set implements the distinction. Whether such a check is buildable is not established.
- **Whether structured status-update templates actually reduce aggregation decay in practice.** The mechanism is plausible across all three outputs; none has measured the effect.
- **Whether the ten considerations identified are the right or complete set.** The matrices are constructed from the brief's framing; no output tests whether different considerations would change the genre rankings.
- **The primary-source content of ISO 24495-1:2023, the US Army writing standard, Minto's original text, Hotaling's original rules, or the SRE postmortem handbook.** Output 1 explicitly marks these as absent from its supplied materials and assigns question marks or unsupported labels accordingly. Outputs 2 and 3 draw on publicly described principles for these techniques, which introduces a potential asymmetry in how confidently claims about those techniques can be made.
