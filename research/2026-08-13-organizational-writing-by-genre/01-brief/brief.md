Task
Test the claim that clear writing is clear writing — that the same principles serve organizational communication regardless of genre — and replace it with something usable. Organizational writing is the case the prior investigations did not cover: every genre in it is a decision genre, where facts are communicated so that somebody acts, chooses, funds, trusts, or forgives. Examine how the available techniques serve or damage ten organizational genres, and produce two matrices plus a decision procedure. TECHNIQUES to cover: ASD-STE100; BLUF and the US Army writing standard; Minto's Pyramid Principle; the Amazon six-page narrative memo and PR-FAQ (including its explicit ban on bullet points, which is a rival theory of clarity to compression, not a stylistic quirk); plain language per ISO 24495-1:2023; blameless postmortem convention as practised in SRE culture; classical and Toulmin-style argumentation; readability-formula optimisation as embodied by the Hemingway Editor; Hotaling's rules for concise scientific writing; and the three skills in github.com/nonatofabio/claude-writing-skills (ste, plainspoken, humanize). GENRES: informational brief (nobody is being asked to decide); investigation summary; research proposal; recommendation and next steps; incident notification written while facts are still moving; postmortem written after; recurring status update; decision record explaining why X was chosen over Y for a reader who was not there; dissent arguing against a decision or a colleague; and reference or onboarding documentation. Pay specific attention to three tensions. First, that an instruction to force claims that commit is a defect in any genre where the honest state is uncertainty — a surface-feature audit cannot distinguish a weasel word from a confidence interval. Second, that incident notification and postmortem are the same event in incompatible registers. Third, that status updates degrade in honesty as each layer of a hierarchy summarises the last, which is a writing problem and an incentive problem at once — say which parts craft can address.

Context
Third in a series, commissioned by DHK, who builds Claude writing skills and writes publicly. The prior investigations are published at dhk/alexandria: 2026-08-12-writing-communication-best-practices established the literature and evaluated the claude-writing-skills repo against it; 2026-08-13-is-ste100-the-right-tool established STE's provenance from sources and found that STE is not a general-purpose writing standard and is actively harmful for persuasion, because it removes by design the devices persuasion depends on. That run also found one assumption shared across STE, the Hemingway Editor, Hotaling's rules and the Claude skills — that surface features proxy quality and that shorter is generally better — and judged it valid as a diagnostic and invalid as an optimisation target, all three models reaching for Goodhart's Law unprompted. This brief extends that finding into the setting where most professional writing actually happens. Downstream: a proposed set of extensions to the claude-writing-skills repo, offered to its maintainer as a collaboration, and DHK's own practice. Where this run contradicts the earlier ones, say so directly and explain which is better supported — a correction is more useful than consistency.

Constraints


Output needs
A document that stands alone for a reader who has seen neither this brief nor the prior runs, in this order. (1) THE VERDICT on whether one set of principles serves all organizational writing, stated up front. (2) MATRIX A — THE DEMAND: rows are the ten considerations below, columns are the ten genres, and each cell states how much that genre requires that quality, on an explicit declared scale with a legend. This matrix is about the situation, not about any tool. (3) MATRIX B — THE SUPPLY: rows are the same ten considerations, columns are the techniques, and each cell states whether that technique serves, is neutral toward, or actively damages that quality, on the same declared scale. Mark cells where the technique's effect is asserted by its proponents but unsupported by evidence. (4) THE SELECTION PROCEDURE, derived by matching a genre's demand profile in Matrix A against the technique profiles in Matrix B — show the matching rule, do not merely assert conclusions, and name where two techniques should be combined or sequenced. (5) THE GENRES WHERE EVERY AVAILABLE TECHNIQUE FAILS, if any, and what would be needed. (6) EVIDENCE AUDIT: for each technique, whether its claims rest on experimental evidence, institutional adoption, practitioner consensus, or folklore — marked per claim, not per technique. (7) WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS ANSWER. The ten considerations, which are fixed and must be used as given: precision; brevity; scannability; actionability; confidence calibration, meaning conveying certainty and uncertainty faithfully rather than sounding assured; empathy and regard for the reader's stake; persuasive force; accountability, meaning whether agency is visible or hidden by agentless passive constructions; accessibility for second-language readers and translation; and durability, meaning legibility to a reader who arrives later without the surrounding context. Note explicitly where these considerations are in tension with one another — brevity against durability, precision against persuasive force — because a technique that maximises all ten does not exist and a matrix that suggests otherwise is wrong.
