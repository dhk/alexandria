# The neighbourhoods of South of Market, and the names that replaced each other

**Status:** analysis, single-analyst, not graded. Every quotation below was
retrieved with `02-run-plan/quote.py` against documents recorded in
`04-normalized/sources/manifest.json`, and every page is as that tool formed it.
**Marker:** `FETCHED` throughout, except where marked otherwise.

## The finding: one ground, five names in eighty years

South of Market's historical neighbourhoods are not, for the most part, a set of
adjacent districts. They are a **succession** — the same few blocks renamed as
what happened on them changed. That is the thing this investigation was
originally about, and the corpus now documents it end to end.

| era | name | what drove the name |
|---|---|---|
| Gold Rush | **Happy Valley**, **Pleasant Valley** | sheltered valleys in the dunes, squatter tent settlements |
| from 1854 | **Tar Flat** | by-products of the Donahue Brothers' gas works |
| cable-car era | **South of the Slot** | the slotted track down the centre of Market Street |
| 20th c. | **South of Market** → **SoMa** | administrative |
| current | **Western SoMa**, **Central SoMa**, **SoMa Pilipinas** | plan areas and heritage districts |

The pivot is explicit in the record:

> The increasing dominance of heavy industry in the South of Market Area
> gradually displaced the bucolic Gold Rush-era neighborhoods of Happy Valley
> and Pleasant Valley. Increasingly, the neighborhood became known by another
> name: "Tar Flat," the result of by-products generated by the Donahue Brothers'
> gas works located at the corner of 1st and Howard streets. Built in 1854 …

— `soma-context-2009`, printed p.30 (PDF p.32).

And the next replacement, with the two names briefly coexisting:

> The character of the South of Market Area, still called "Tar Flat," or
> increasingly "South of the Slot," in reference to the Market Street Railway
> cable car tracks running down the center of Market Street …

— `soma-context-2009`, printed p.30 (PDF p.32).

So *Tar Flat* is not a neighbour of *Happy Valley*. It is Happy Valley, later.
An atlas that draws them as two polygons side by side is drawing something that
never existed.

## The named places, with extents where the record gives one

| name | extent as stated | source |
|---|---|---|
| **Happy Valley** | "a protected valley in the middle of the sand dunes bounded by Market, Howard, 1st, and 2nd streets" | `soma-context-2009`, printed p.20 |
| **Pleasant Valley** | "another valley to the south bounded by Howard, Folsom, 1st, and 2nd streets" | `soma-context-2009`, printed p.20 |
| **Tar Flat** | no bounds given; succeeds Happy + Pleasant Valley | `soma-context-2009`, printed p.30 |
| **South of the Slot** | the whole area south of Market, by contrast with north of it | `soma-context-2009`, printed p.30 |
| **Steamboat Point** | "the now disused name … for the area along the north side of Mission Creek"; boatyards at the foot of 1st Street from 1851 | `market-octavia-context-2007`, printed p.31 |
| **Rincon Hill** | "San Francisco's most desirable address until construction of the Second Street Cut in 1869" | `central-soma-deir-cultural`, printed IV.C-6 |
| **South Park** | George Gordon began buying lots in 1852 for a townhouse development around an oval garden | `central-soma-deir-cultural`, printed IV.C-6 |
| **Mission Bay** | the marsh and mudflats "stretched north to Market Street and west to Mission Street" before filling from c.1850 | `central-soma-deir-cultural`, printed IV.C-5 |
| **SoMa Pilipinas** | a Filipino social heritage district; a 2011 task force proposed a Special Use District | `soma-filipino-heritage-2013` |

Two of these are bounded well enough to become polygons without invention —
Happy Valley and Pleasant Valley — and they abut: Market–Howard and
Howard–Folsom, both between 1st and 2nd. That mutual consistency is itself
evidence, and it decides the conflict below.

## Conflict: the extent of Happy Valley

Two documents in this corpus disagree, and the disagreement is exactly the kind
that a gazetteer must resolve rather than average.

**A.** "A protected valley in the middle of the sand dunes **bounded by Market,
Howard, 1st, and 2nd streets** soon became known as 'Happy Valley'"
— `soma-context-2009`, printed p.20 (PDF p.22).

**B.** "the majority of development south of Market Street was concentrated in
'Happy Valley,' **located along the shoreline — approximately First Street —
between Market and Mission Streets**"
— `central-soma-deir-cultural`, printed IV.C-5 (PDF p.5).

They differ on the southern edge: **Howard** in A, **Mission** in B. Mission
lies between Market and Howard, so B's valley is roughly half of A's, and set
one block further east.

**Adjudicated in favour of A**, on three grounds, all internal to the record:

1. **A is the dedicated context statement**; B is the cultural-resources chapter
   of an EIR for a different plan area, summarising background.
2. **A is coherent with its neighbour.** The same sentence in A continues into
   Pleasant Valley "bounded by Howard, Folsom, 1st, and 2nd" — the two valleys
   share the Howard Street edge and stack cleanly. Under B, Happy Valley stops
   at Mission and a block of nobody's-land opens between the two.
3. **A gives four streets; B gives an approximation** ("approximately First
   Street"), and says so itself.

This is an adjudication, not a measurement. It is `SEARCH-SUMMARY`-grade
reasoning over two `FETCHED` quotations, and a third document could overturn it.

## What is modern administration, not history

DataSF's analysis neighbourhoods give **South of Market**, **Mission Bay** and
**Financial District/South Beach** as polygons. None of them is Happy Valley,
Tar Flat or Steamboat Point, and none should be labelled with those names.
`04-normalized/geo/neighborhoods-sources.json` says this on its own face. The
modern polygons are a frame; the names above are the history hung on it.

## What this is not

- **A complete list.** It is the SoMa names that these eleven documents write
  down. Absence here is not absence from the record.
- **Extents.** Seven of the nine names have no bounds in this corpus at all.
  Turning "the area along the north side of Mission Creek" into a polygon needs
  the prose-to-polygon work that is not built.
- **Dated by proximity.** No date here was taken from a nearby number. Each is
  asserted by the sentence quoted. The earlier bulk extraction that dated plats
  by "nearest four-digit year" returned a man's birth year as a plat date; that
  method is not used here.
- **Independent.** `soma-context-2009` and `central-soma-deir-cultural` are both
  SF Planning products and the EIR cites work of the same authors, so agreement
  between them is weaker evidence than it looks. The one place they *disagree*
  is therefore the more interesting.
