What the slider is actually doing

Every street on this map is a federal centreline, assigned to a survey grid by the direction it runs. Three of those grids can be dated from the sources in this project; the streets belonging to them appear on their survey year and not before.

The dates are events rather than processes, so the map jumps. O'Farrell's survey did not fade in over a decade — it was drawn, and then it existed. Smoothing that into a gradient would be a prettier animation and a false one.

What the map cannot show is construction. A survey is a plan on paper; the streets it specifies were built over the decades that followed, and many of the lines that appear in 1868 were sand until the 1900s. This is a timeline of when the city was laid out, not when it was built.

Why it stops in 1868

Not because the city stopped being surveyed — because this corpus stops being able to date it. Four of the seven grids on this map have no survey date here, and a third of the city's street length belongs to no grid at all: the hills, the parks, the Presidio, the deliberately curving plats of Sea Cliff and St Francis Wood.

An earlier version of this page let the slider run to 2026 so you could feel the silence. It turns out that a hundred and fifty-eight years of nothing happening is just a hundred and fifty-eight years of nothing happening. The gap is better stated than endured, so the timeline ends where its evidence does and the panel beside the map carries the rest.

Together that is 45% of San Francisco, present on the map and absent from its history. The honest way to draw a gap is at full size.

This is patchwork, on purpose

The provenance here will change as people who know more say so. Homestead associations and subdivision tracts are the missing unit — the Eureka Homestead Association of 1864, the Market Street Homestead Association of 1868, dozens more named in the planning record — each one a dated, bounded plat that would fill the space between the three city-scale surveys. They are not on this map yet because each date has to be read out of a sentence and checked, not scraped.
