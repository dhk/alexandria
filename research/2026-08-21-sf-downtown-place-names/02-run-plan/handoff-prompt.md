# Research brief — San Francisco place names: three unresolved questions

Self-contained. You need no other document to answer this.

## What this is for

A version-controlled historical gazetteer of San Francisco place names for
the downtown / South of Market district, 1847 to the present. It records how
names emerged, shifted, competed, faded and were revived, and it will later
feed a map with a time slider.

The governing principle: **a neighborhood is not a timeless polygon. It is a
claim, made by a particular source, at a particular date, with a particular
degree of geographic precision and social or administrative authority.** Every
answer should be shaped that way — not "where is X", but "who said X was
where, when, and how much weight does that carry".

Three questions remain unresolved after a first research pass. They are the
ones that pure retrieval might settle and that earlier work could not.

## Already established — do not spend effort re-deriving

A prior pass read SF Planning's *Historic Context Statement, South of the
Market Area* (Page & Turnbull, Inc., 30 June 2009, 118 pp.), and it settles
the following. Treat these as known:

- The 100 Vara Survey name came from O'Farrell laying out each block south of
  Market as six lots of 100 varas square; a vara is about 33 inches (printed p.2, note 3 — PDF page 4).
- The 1869 Second Street Cut was built by private investors, ostensibly to
  improve access from Market Street to the Pacific Mail Wharf at 1st and
  Brannan, and was "crudely blasted through the center of Rincon Hill",
  damaging property and ruining the hill's desirability (printed p.34 — PDF page 36).
- Happy Valley was "bounded by Market, Howard, 1st, and 2nd streets" and named
  by pioneer miners (printed p.20 — PDF page 22).
- Pleasant Valley lay just south of Happy Valley and is described as "later
  called" that (printed p.17 — PDF page 19).
- Steamboat Point took its name from boatyards at the foot of 1st Street, "as
  early as 1851" (printed p.20 — PDF page 22).
- Tar Flat displaced Happy Valley and Pleasant Valley as the area's name,
  after by-products from the Donahue Brothers' gas works at 1st and Howard,
  built 1854 (printed p.30 — PDF page 32).
- Rincon Point is a headland sheltering Yerba Buena Cove, distinct from Rincon
  Hill.

## The three questions

### Q1. When does "SoMa" first appear in print?

The hardest and most valuable. Establish the **earliest datable published use
of "SoMa" / "SOMA"** as a name for the San Francisco district — newspaper,
magazine, planning document, real-estate listing, gallery notice, anything
dated and citable.

Also address:

- **Who put it into print**, and in what register — journalist, developer,
  arts venue, planner, resident.
- **Whether it was coined in imitation of SoHo in New York.** This is widely
  repeated and, as far as the prior pass could tell, sourced to nobody. Either
  find who first made the claim, or report that it circulates unattributed.
- **Whether its extent at first use matched "South of Market".** Do not assume
  the two are synonyms at any date. The acronym existing is not evidence that
  a neighborhood by that name existed.

Note: SF Planning's own 118-page context statement uses "SoMa" ten times and
**every one is a modern planning label** ("Western SoMa Light Industrial and
Residential Historic District"). It never dates the name. If you cannot fix an
earliest use either, say so plainly and give the earliest you *can* verify —
that is a useful result, and far better than a confident guess.

### Q2. The East Cut — the adoption record

A community benefit district adopted "The East Cut" as a neighborhood name in
the late 2010s. Establish:

- **The exact year and mechanism.** Reporting points at **2017**; some sources
  say 2018. Pin it. The body was previously the Greater Rincon Hill CBD.
- **The stated boundaries**, in street names, as the CBD itself published them.
- **The stated historical derivation** — it refers to the 1869 Second Street
  Cut. Quote the CBD's own wording.
- **The reaction**, including from residents of the older names it overlays
  (Rincon Hill, South Beach, Transbay, SoMa). A 2018 survey reportedly found
  ~90% of residents thought the name was "dumb" — find the actual survey, who
  ran it, sample size, and the question asked, or report that you couldn't.
- **Whether anyone beyond the coining body ever adopted it.** The name
  reportedly disappeared from Google and Apple Maps around September 2024.
  Confirm or correct, with dates.

Treat the CBD's own materials as a primary source about *its intentions*, not
as a source about the district.

### Q3. The 100 Vara District's extent — a live conflict

Two accounts disagree and both need checking:

- **Page & Turnbull (2009), printed p.18 (PDF page 20)**, citing *The Original and Authentic Plan of
  San Francisco* (1847): "From Yerba Buena Cove west to 1st Street, the street
  grid replicated the 50 Vara Survey north of Market Street but from 1st
  Street west to 5th Street, O'Farrell adhered to the larger blocks of the
  100-Vara Survey."
- **A 1945 city document**, as reported secondhand: the 100 Vara District runs
  "from the south side of Market Street to the Ferry", with the South of
  Market area "officially known as 100 Vara".

These are materially different extents for the same name. Find the 1847 plan
and the 1945 document if you can. **Do not resolve the disagreement by
picking the more popular answer** — report both with their evidence, say which
is better supported and why, and if you cannot adjudicate it, say that.

## Rules for your answer

1. **Link every factual claim** to a specific retrievable source — title,
   creator or publisher, date, and URL or archive identifier. A claim with no
   locator is not usable here.
2. **Mark each source as read or not read.** If you retrieved and read the
   page, say so. If you are working from a search snippet or from memory, say
   that instead. Do not blur the two.
3. **Separate contemporaneous evidence from retrospective commentary.** An
   1899 map labelling an area and a 2020 article describing what people called
   it in 1899 are different classes of evidence. Mark which is which per
   claim, not once at the end.
4. **Do not manufacture dates.** Give ranges where evidence is partial and say
   what bounds them.
5. **Do not give a colloquial name a precise boundary** it has no evidence
   for. "Unknown extent" is a correct answer.
6. **Treat branding and real-estate language as evidence of what its author
   wanted**, never as neutral fact.
7. **Report what you could not find**, specifically enough to act on — which
   database, which years, which publication. A well-specified gap is worth
   more than a padded answer.

## Output format

For each of Q1, Q2, Q3, in order:

- **Verdict** — one paragraph: what the evidence establishes and how firmly.
  If it is unsettled, say so here, not buried below.
- **Evidence** — the dated claims with their sources and locators inline.
- **What you could not verify.**

Then close with:

- **Source list** — everything you used: title, creator, date, URL or
  identifier, whether you read it or only saw it summarised, and one line on
  what that source can and cannot settle.
- **What would change this answer.**

A shorter answer with clean, checkable provenance is worth more than a
comprehensive one that cannot be verified.
