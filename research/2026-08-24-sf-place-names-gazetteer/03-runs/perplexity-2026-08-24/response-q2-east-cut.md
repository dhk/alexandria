## **Verdict**

**"The East Cut" was adopted by the Greater Rincon Hill Community Benefit District in 2017 through a privately commissioned naming-and-branding process, not through a 2017 or 2018 City neighborhood-naming action or renewal of the assessment district.** The name was already public by **June 1, 2017**, and the CBD formally launched its new identity on its website on **June 8, 2017**. The available record says the CBD board selected it after considering alternatives, but I could not retrieve the decisive 2017 board minutes or establish an exact vote date; one December 2017 report says the name was created "in May." Google Maps' adoption in **spring 2018** explains the recurrent 2018 date. The City continued to call the legal assessment district "Greater Rincon Hill CBD, dba The East Cut" in 2018 and did not formally change the district's name until **Board of Supervisors Resolution 492-19 on November 19, 2019**. Thus, 2017 is firm for the branding adoption; June 8 is the firm CBD launch date; the exact board-vote date remains unproved.

## **Evidence**

### 1. Adoption year and mechanism

- **June 1, 2017 — contemporaneous; READ.** SFist reported that the then–Greater Rincon Hill CBD was spending assessment revenue on a branding campaign comprising a new name, logo, website and branded employee clothing; Executive Director Andrew Robinson described "The East Cut" as "a 21st century idea of what a neighborhood should be." This establishes that the name and campaign were operational no later than June 1, but it is reporting, not the authorizing record. **Weight: medium.**

- **June 8, 2017 — contemporaneous primary evidence about the CBD's intention; READ.** In **"Introducing The East Cut,"** the CBD publicly announced: "In selecting The East Cut we have created a confident name layered with both heritage and potential," and displayed its "new logo." It characterized the result as a "community effort" involving community meetings, interviews and public board meetings. This is the clearest dated **launch document**, but it does not identify a resolution, motion, vote date or vote tally. **Weight: high for public launch; low for the precise corporate authorization.**

- **August 4, 2017 — contemporaneous primary evidence about process; READ.** The CBD said it had "collectively decided to embark on a thoughtful and meaningful neighborhood identity and naming process." It listed historical and planning-document review, a community meeting, walking tours, stakeholder interviews, street interviews, review of MLS listing names, and "numerous publicly-accessible Committee and Board meetings." Crucially, it then said: **"Our process specifically was to determine a name for the CBD itself, and not change the name of any existing neighborhoods within the district's larger boundaries."** That qualification conflicts with the June launch page's repeated description of The East Cut as a neighborhood. **Weight: high for the CBD's stated justification; not proof of representative neighborhood consent.**

- **December 14, 2017 — contemporaneous reporting; NOT READ, search summary only.** The *San Francisco Business Times* reported that the CBD "created the name in May," tying together Transbay, Rincon Hill and parts of SoMa. Because I could not read the full article or identify the underlying May meeting, **May 2017 is a plausible bound, not an exact adoption date**.

- **August 2–5, 2018 — retrospective reporting; READ.** The *New York Times* reported that the CBD board rejected alternatives including **"Grand Narrows"** and **"Central Hub"** and selected "The East Cut"; it also reported that Google added the name in spring 2018 after the CBD requested it. This supports a board-level selection in 2017 but provides no meeting date or minutes. **Weight: medium.**

- **June–September 2018 — contemporaneous administrative record; NOT READ, official search summary only.** City legislation still styled the organization **"Greater Rincon Hill Community Benefit District (dba The East Cut Community Benefit District)"** in the FY2016–17 annual-report proceeding. That means the branding/DBA existed, but the legal assessment-district name had not yet been changed. **Weight: high for legal nomenclature.**

- **November 19, 2019 — contemporaneous City action reconstructed from official records; READ.** The Board of Supervisors approved the FY2017–18 reports and changed the district name to **The East Cut Community Benefit District** in **Resolution No. 492-19**. This was the formal City recognition of the assessment district's new name—not the original neighborhood-brand launch. **Weight: high for the legal district name, but it does not establish an official City neighborhood polygon.**

- **Not a 2017–18 renewal.** The governing **June/July 2015 Greater Rincon Hill CBD Management Plan** established a 15-year assessment term through FY2029–30. The 2017 branding did not redraw or renew that plan. **Weight: high; governing assessment document.**

### 2. Boundaries claimed by the CBD, versus the assessment district

- **2015 legal assessment geography — contemporaneous primary document; READ.** The management plan describes an irregular parcel-based district. In street terms, its perimeter runs principally: east side of **Second Street**, beginning at **Jessie Street**, south to **Harrison Street** and an adjoining parcel; **Harrison Street** from Second to **Main Street**, with listed parcel exclusions; Main south to **Bryant Street**; Bryant east to **The Embarcadero**; the west side of the Embarcadero back to Harrison; **Steuart Street** from Harrison to **Howard Street**; Howard to **Spear Street**; Spear to **Mission Street**; Mission to **First Street**; then an irregular northern return through portions of First, **Stevenson Street**, **Ecker Street**, and specified parcel lines back to Second. See Management Plan p. 25 and Engineer's Report p. 13. **Weight: controlling for assessed parcels, not for a vernacular neighborhood.**

- **June 2017 public claim — contemporaneous reporting; READ.** Curbed described the proposed East Cut as starting at Second Street and covering Rincon Hill, three blocks of the Embarcadero, and the Transbay district from **Jessie Street to the Bay Bridge entrance**. This was a journalistic paraphrase of the branding geography, not a legal survey. **Weight: medium-low.**

- **Current CBD neighborhood claim — primary evidence about present intention; READ, page undated.** The CBD's current "District Map" page says the neighborhood spans **Market Street to Harrison Street north–south and Second Street to Steuart Street east–west**. This rectangular description is substantially simpler and extends farther north than much of the parcel-based 2015 assessment perimeter. **Weight: high for what the CBD currently wants "The East Cut" to mean; low as legal or social proof.**

- **Current CBD FAQ — primary evidence, READ, and internally inconsistent.** The FAQ instead says the boundaries are "the east side of 2nd Street to The Embarcadero, and the North side of Mission to Harrison streets," and immediately states that the boundaries are determined by parcels paying the CBD assessment. That does not match the same organization's Market-to-Harrison "neighborhood" rectangle and compresses an irregular parcel perimeter into a rough description. **Conclusion:** the CBD has not consistently separated its broader neighborhood brand from its assessment-service territory.

### 3. CBD's stated historical derivation

- **June 8, 2017 — contemporaneous primary source; READ.** The CBD's own launch wording was:

  > "The name, The East Cut is inspired by the Second Street Cut, a defining moment in our history. But, the name also points to our role as the epicenter of the bustling eastern side of the city."

  It later added:

  > "The name was inspired by the Second Street Cut, the leveling of Rincon Hill which both created out current southern border and defined the character of the neighborhood for almost 100 years."

- **August 4, 2017 — contemporaneous primary source; READ.** The CBD described the name as referring to **"the district's geographical location east of the Second Street Cut."**

- **Current CBD history — retrospective primary source about the CBD's narrative; READ.** Its present wording is:

  > "Then in 1869, city planners decided to flatten the center of Rincon Hill along 2nd Street, creating the 2nd Street Cut. While the Cut facilitated commerce between downtown and the docks at South Beach, the change scarred the neighborhood and it lost its cachet."

- I did **not** find CBD wording calling it a **"private cut," "blasted"**, or expressly naming the **Pacific Mail Wharf**. A separate historical account says landowner John Middleton promoted a flat route to the Pacific Mail wharves to improve Second Street's commercial appeal and his property value, but that is retrospective historical commentary, not the CBD's derivation statement.

### 4. Resident reaction and the reported "90%" survey

- **June 2017 — contemporaneous reaction; READ.** Rincon Hill resident Lauri Mashoian objected: **"I don't know why they want to rebrand Rincon Hill, which is real and historic and accurate… If you have to explain something, maybe it's not right."**

- **June 2017 — contemporaneous reaction; READ.** Longtime Rincon Hill resident Jamie Whitaker said residents were only beginning to distinguish Rincon Hill from South Beach and hoped people would continue using Rincon Hill south of Folsom. He called East Cut "okay" but questioned whether it would endure beyond its sponsor.

- **August 2018 — contemporaneous reaction; READ.** ABC7 recorded mixed or confused responses: Veronica Chung used the new name, while Micah Brown said he preferred retaining the historic name; the report characterized workers and residents as perplexed. **Weight: illustrative interviews, not representative sampling.**

- **The actual survey remains unlocated.** The *New York Times* reported only that **Tad Bogdan**, a 14-year resident, had organized a survey of **271 neighbors** and **"he said, 90 percent disliked the name."** It did not reproduce the instrument, exact question, answer choices, recruitment method, raw counts, field dates or publication URL. "Thought the name was dumb" is later paraphrase; **"disliked the name"** is the closest retrievable wording.

- **The date is not securely 2018.** On August 4, 2017, the CBD acknowledged that a non-board-sanctioned "survey" had recently circulated, but it did not name Bogdan or give a sample size. The NYT called Bogdan's survey "recent" in August 2018. These could be the same survey, but the evidence does not establish that; its field date is therefore bounded only between the June 2017 launch and the August 2, 2018 NYT report.

- **Authority weight:** even if accurately reported, 271 self-selected or otherwise undocumented respondents cannot establish the opinion of all residents. It is evidence that Bogdan obtained strong opposition among his respondents, not a scientifically documented 90% neighborhood consensus. The missing instrument prevents stronger interpretation.

## **What you could not verify**

1. **The decisive 2017 board record.** I could not retrieve an agenda, minute, resolution or roll-call vote stating: "The board adopts The East Cut," nor establish whether the selection occurred at one meeting or by staff/committee consensus later ratified. The CBD's present "Public Meetings" archive did not expose 2017 documents through the retrieved page.
2. **An exact May 2017 date.** The *San Francisco Business Times* search summary says the name was created "in May," but the underlying article and cited meeting record were not available here.
3. **The original survey.** I did not find an instrument or result file in: the South Beach/Rincon/Mission Bay Neighborhood Association's **Surveys** page; its indexed 2017–18 meeting materials; the Rincon Hill neighborhood blog's indexed 2018 archive; the CBD's own 2017 response; NYT and CNBC versions of the 2018 article.
4. **Survey wording, choices and denominator details.** Only "271 neighbors" and "90 percent disliked the name," attributed by the NYT to Bogdan, are retrievable. The frequently repeated wording **"thought it was dumb"** is not the documented question or result.
5. **Exact Google or Apple removal dates.** September 26, 2024 is the first located press observation, not a platform change log.
6. **A formal MLS taxonomy decision.** Individual listings prove use by landlords, agents or syndicators; they do not establish that SFAR MLS or another MLS governing body created an East Cut neighborhood code.
7. **USPS adoption.** No East Cut entry was found in the publicly indexed USPS/PostalPro material searched; no claim of affirmative USPS recognition can be made from that absence.
