## **Verdict**

The **earliest datable published district-name use I could verify is 6 November 1988**: the *Los Angeles Times* described San Francisco's "warehouse district, nicknamed **SoMa (for south of Market)**." This is a contemporaneous, independent journalistic use, but only **moderate evidence for priority**: its date is encoded in the archive URL, no byline or page number is displayed, and inaccessible or incompletely searchable San Francisco archives may contain earlier examples. Reports that Ali Ghanbarian began *SOMA* magazine in **1986 or 1987** make that serial the leading earlier candidate, but I did not retrieve a dated first issue and therefore **cannot fix the first printed use earlier than 6 November 1988**.

## **Evidence**

- **6 November 1988 — contemporaneous; READ.** "Warehouse District Caters to Budget-Minded," *Los Angeles Times*, uncredited, archive URL dated 1988-11-06: "The warehouse district, nicknamed **SoMa (for south of Market)**, is an area of factory buildings bordered by Market, 1st, Townsend and 12th streets." **Register:** anonymous journalist, in a travel/retail feature—not developer or resident testimony. **Weight:** strongest verified early use; independent and explicit, although not demonstrably first. https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-11-06-tr-170-story.html

- **1986–1987 — retrospective; READ, but original issue NOT READ.** Ali Ghanbarian's biographical page reproduces an unidentified clipping saying he "began publishing [*SOMA*] in **1986**"; *SOMA*'s press page reproduces a 21 May 2009 *Magazine Rack* article saying the magazine was first published "22 years ago," implying **1987**. Because neither page supplies a first-issue date, volume/number, scan, or stable library identifier, the defensible bound is **1986–1987**, not a precise date. **Register:** publisher/restaurateur's arts-and-nightlife branding. **Weight:** plausible earlier candidate, weak for establishing first district-name publication.

- **29 November 1989 — contemporaneous; READ.** *San Francisco Bay Guardian*, vol. 24, no. 8, used "**SOMA nightspots/restaurants**" in a Potrero Court Apartments advertisement, while elsewhere retaining "South of Market." This independently shows generic/commercial currency by late 1989; as advertising, it evidences the advertiser's intended geography rather than a neutral boundary. Internet Archive identifier **Issue24.08**, pp. 87 and 33–36. https://archive.org/stream/Issue24.08/Issue24.08_djvu.txt

- **Initial extent — contemporaneous; READ.** The 1988 *Times* writer's **SoMa** meant the stated warehouse area bounded by Market, First, Townsend and Twelfth—not everything geographically south of Market. That is only this writer's referent; it does not establish a generally accepted boundary.

- **SoHo derivation — retrospective; READ.** The earliest retrieved attributable comparison is Ali Ghanbarian's 4 June 2009 statement that the "artsy-warehouse district" was "similar to **SoHo in the '60s**." He did **not** say there that the word *SoMa* was coined by imitating *SoHo*. The stronger proposition therefore remains **circulating but unattributed** in the sources examined; I found no first claimant with a dated primary locator. "Seven Questions with Ali Ghanbarian," Samir Husni, *Mr. Magazine*, 4 June 2009.

- **Coinage claim — retrospective; READ.** Ghanbarian's current biography says he "coined the term SoMa," but the page is dated 2023, supplies no contemporary document, and is self-promotional.
