The available evidence supports **"50 Vara" and "100 Vara" as historical San Francisco survey/block-designation terms that appear in block books and assessor block-book materials**. It does **not**, from search snippets alone, establish that every phrase such as "100 Vara Block 12" was a complete, operative deed description; the underlying deeds and page images must be inspected before making that conclusion.

## Findings

**1. Recorded/block-book usage**

- The 1909–1910 *San Francisco Original Handy Block Book* is described as covering both the "Fifty Vara Survey" and "One Hundred Vara Survey," with block sizes, lots, and owners compiled from official records. **Status: NOT READ—search snippet only.** [S1]
- San Francisco's current digitized assessor block-book PDFs still label pages "50 VARA BLK." followed by a block number—for example, block 250 and block 169. These are official-looking assessor block-book materials, but the supplied results do not establish whether they are current legal descriptions or historical cross-reference labels. **Status: NOT READ—search snippet only.** [S2][S3]
- The search results therefore support the narrower conclusion that **"50 Vara Block" is a real historical and continuing assessor/block-book designation**. They do not, without reviewing deeds or page images, prove that the exact forms "100 Vara Block N" or "50 Vara Survey Block N" were universally used as standalone deed descriptions. [S1][S2][S3]

**2. Geographic extent of the 100 Vara series**

- The SF Memory block-book description says the "One Hundred Vara Survey" covers South-of-Market blocks **from Ninth Street to the Bay**. **Status: NOT READ—search snippet only.** [S4]
- A separate historical summary identifies the 100 Vara District as extending **from the south side of Market Street to the Ferry**. **Status: NOT READ—search snippet only; secondary source.** [S5]
- These descriptions are not identical: "Ninth Street to the Bay" gives a more southern/western block-series description, while "south side of Market Street to the Ferry" describes a broader South-of-Market area. The sources should therefore be treated as **different definitions or extents**, not reconciled without inspecting the underlying maps or city documents. [S4][S5]

**3. When the system began and whether it remains current**

- The historical summary dates the official name "100 Vara Survey" to **1847**. **Status: NOT READ—search snippet only; secondary source.** [S5]
- The surviving block books demonstrate use of the 50 Vara and 100 Vara designations by at least **1901, 1906, and 1909–1910**. [S1][S6][S7]
- San Francisco Public Library's research guide says the Assessor-Recorder's ownership and transfer records run from **1914 to the present** and directs researchers to the Property Information Map for current block-and-lot numbers. [S8]
- The evidence indicates that the Vara surveys are **historical survey/block systems retained in assessor reference materials**, while present-day parcel identification uses the modern **Assessor's Block and Lot** system. The supplied snippets do not document a single formal repeal or replacement date. [S2][S3][S8]
