Task
Produce a research-led brief on written-communication best practices, then use it to evaluate and propose extensions to the claude-writing-skills repo (github.com/nonatofabio/claude-writing-skills). Do NOT start from the repo. Start from the external evidence: what does the research and practitioner literature say are best practices for writing, and how do those best practices vary by context, purpose, audience, and content type? Relevant bodies of knowledge include controlled-language/technical-writing standards (STE100/ASD-STE100), plain-language guidelines, technical writing and style standards, readability research, and current research/discourse on the utility, risks, and detectability tradeoffs of clearly-AI-generated text (vs. text engineered to pass as human-written). Only after establishing that synthesis, turn to the repo's three skills (ste, plainspoken, humanize) and assess fit, coverage, and gaps against it. Conclude with a prioritized, evidence-grounded set of proposed extensions intended as a collaborative pitch to the repo's maintainer.

Context
Commissioned by DHK (Head of Data, Infinitus Systems), who builds Claude skills and writes publicly (Substack: dhkondata.substack.com) using a git-native skills workflow. He intends this brief as the evidence base for a set of proposed extensions to the repo, framed as a collaboration with its maintainer — so the research synthesis needs to stand on its own as a credible, citable literature review, independent of the tool, before the tool is evaluated against it. He wants an opinionated, citation-backed analysis, not a generic summary.

Constraints


Output needs
A structured brief, in this order: (1) SYNTHESIS FIRST — a survey of established best practices for written communication, organized by how they vary with context, purpose, audience, and content type. Cover at minimum: controlled-language/technical-writing standards (STE100/ASD-STE100 and comparable controlled-English standards), plain-language guidelines, general style/rhetoric standards, readability research, and the emerging research/practitioner literature on clearly-AI-generated or AI-disclosed text (detectability, reader trust, appropriate use, risks of over-smoothing or false humanization). Each claim cited to a specific source. This section should be a standalone literature synthesis, not tool-specific. (2) TOOL EVALUATION SECOND — only after the synthesis, assess the claude-writing-skills repo's three skills (ste, plainspoken, humanize) against that synthesis: where do they implement recognized best practice correctly, where do they diverge or oversimplify, where do they omit context/purpose/audience-sensitivity that the research says matters. (3) GAP ANALYSIS — explicit strengths, weaknesses, and product gaps of the tool relative to the synthesis. (4) PROPOSED EXTENSIONS — a prioritized set of concrete extensions (new skills, rule changes, structural changes) framed as a collaborative proposal to the maintainer, each tied to a specific cited finding from part 1, not asserted without support.
