# Synthesis: Supersimple Custom Apps — bundling, not a new technical capability

Read charitably, Supersimple's differentiation claim is not "we invented custom apps in
BI" — it's that no existing tool packages governed-model access, external write-back,
org-wide reuse, and AI-artifact governance into one admin-controlled surface. Individually,
every capability in the transcript has a live precedent: Sigma and Tableau already treat
custom code as a first-class, shareable canvas object; Looker already has the cleanest
governed-model-plus-external-backend-plus-entitlement story; several tools already support
some form of write-back. What's missing from the comparison set is any single tool that
does all of it at once, in one governed surface.

That makes this a bundling and go-to-market differentiator, not a technical one — real and
defensible if the shipped feature actually delivers all three legs, but the kind of claim a
competitor can contest by pointing at their own piece of the bundle rather than by disputing
any individual fact.

The framing most worth repeating externally with confidence is the "shadow AI-coding" one:
no comparison vendor has yet positioned its extension capability as the sanctioned home for
agent-built one-off artifacts, even though the underlying governance problem is a live,
widely-discussed category. The two claims most worth product-side scrutiny before repeating
externally are the shared-filter-state claim (Q2 — not zero-configuration in any comparison
tool today, and the easiest claim for a competitor to challenge with a live demo) and the
"take action in your core systems" framing, where the real differentiator is *where* write-back
sits (inside the same governed permission model as reads) rather than *whether* it exists at
all.

This synthesis rests on one single-source pass with live web search against public
documentation, not hands-on product testing or a second model's independent read. The two
open questions that would most change this read — whether Supersimple's admin controls ship
as granular as Looker's entitlement model, and whether Domo's App Framework already makes a
similar pitch — are exactly what a Silver-tier multi-model dispatch would be positioned to
de-risk. That dispatch has not been run.
